# Research Before Claims

> Never invent a fact to fill a gap — a flagged gap beats a plausible-sounding number.

---

## When to Use

Any time content going into a real deliverable (a website, a document, a pitch) states a
figure, date, quote, source, or specific fact — especially if it's compliance-sensitive,
attributed to a real person/company, or came from a brief that might itself be wrong.

---

## Agent Behaviour

1. **Don't trust a brief at face value if it makes a checkable factual claim.** Specs and
   briefs can be stale, conflate two different things, or overstate — verify independently
   when the claim matters.
2. **Keep three states visibly distinct**: confirmed (a named, reputable source states it
   explicitly), inferred (implied but not stated outright), and not found. Report all three
   honestly — "not found" is a useful, expected answer, not a failure.
3. **Cite what you can.** If a figure has a real source, attribute it. If it doesn't, don't
   publish it dressed up as if it does.
4. **When research contradicts something already shipped or approved**, say so clearly and
   let the person decide what to do about it — don't quietly leave the error in place, and
   don't unilaterally rewrite approved content without flagging the change.
5. **Flag superlatives and absolute claims especially hard** ("the only," "the first," "the
   best") — these are the ones most likely to be marketing framing rather than a fact anyone
   actually attested to.

---

## Quality Bar

- Could every factual sentence survive someone asking "what's the source for that?"
- If two facts seem to contradict each other, did you notice, or did you publish both?
- Did "not found" ever actually get reported, or did every gap quietly get filled?

---

## Anti-Patterns

- Filling a missing number, date, or quote with something plausible-sounding.
- Treating a brief/spec as ground truth when it makes a specific, checkable claim.
- Blurring "a source implies this" into "a source states this."
- Silently correcting or leaving an inaccurate approved claim without telling anyone.
