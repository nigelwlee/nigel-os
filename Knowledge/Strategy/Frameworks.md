# Strategy Frameworks

Mental models and tools. These change slowly. Reference when diagnosing problems, structuring recommendations, or pressure-testing a plan.

---

## Diagnosis & Problem Structuring

**Issue Tree / Logic Tree**
Break a problem into MECE components. Start with the central question, branch into sub-questions, keep splitting until you hit something you can actually research or test. Use for: structuring analysis, splitting work across a team, finding where the answer actually lives.

**5 Whys**
Ask "why" five times to reach the root cause. Prevents solving symptoms. Use for: ops problems, customer complaints, anything that keeps recurring.

**MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive)**
Organise ideas so they don't overlap and don't have gaps. The quality check for any framework you build. If two boxes could contain the same thing, or if something doesn't fit any box, the structure is broken.

## Market & Competitive Analysis

**Porter's Five Forces**
Assess industry attractiveness: Competitive Rivalry, Supplier Power, Buyer Power, Threat of Substitution, Threat of New Entry. Use for: entering a new market, assessing a competitor's moat, deciding where to play.

**2x2 Positioning Matrix**
Plot competitors on two axes that matter to your target customer. Find the white space. Use for: finding an open positioning, communicating differentiation, identifying who you're really competing with.

**TAM / SAM / SOM**
Total Addressable Market → Serviceable Addressable Market → Serviceable Obtainable Market. Use for: sizing an opportunity, stress-testing growth assumptions. Common mistake: TAM is irrelevant; SOM is what matters.

**Jobs to Be Done (JTBD)**
Map the job the customer is trying to get done (functional, social, emotional dimensions). Then map what they currently hire to do it. Find the gap or the frustration. Use for: product positioning, pricing, messaging.

## Decision-Making

**Reversible vs. Irreversible Decisions**
Type 1 (irreversible, high stakes): go slow, get it right. Type 2 (reversible, low stakes): go fast, iterate. Most decisions are Type 2 but get treated like Type 1. Use for: calibrating how much process to apply to any given decision.

**Pre-Mortem**
Before launching, ask: "It's 12 months later and this failed spectacularly. What happened?" Forces identification of real risks before they're real. Use for: any major initiative before committing resources.

**OODA Loop (Boyd)**
Observe → Orient → Decide → Act. The faster your OODA loop vs. a competitor's, the more adaptable you are. Use for: understanding why speed is a structural advantage, diagnosing where you're slowing down.

## Growth & GTM

**Crossing the Chasm Beachhead**
Identify a single, specific market segment where you can achieve 100% penetration before expanding. The beachhead must be: reachable, winnable, and a reference for adjacent markets. Use for: early GTM strategy, deciding where to focus sales.

**Product-Led Growth (PLG) vs. Sales-Led Growth (SLG)**
PLG: product itself acquires, activates, retains users (Slack, Notion). SLG: sales team does the acquiring (enterprise software). Hybrid is common at scale. Use for: deciding go-to-market motion, pricing model, org design.

**The Hook Model (Nir Eyal)**
Trigger → Action → Variable Reward → Investment. Habit-forming products run users through this loop repeatedly. Use for: product design, retention mechanics, understanding engagement.

---

## Operations & Execution

**OKRs (Objectives & Key Results)**
Objective: qualitative, inspiring, memorable. Key Results: 3-5 measurable outcomes that define success. Use for: team alignment, quarterly planning, setting ambitious but grounded targets.

**RACI Matrix**
Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed. One person is Accountable (the throat to choke). Use for: clarifying ownership in any cross-functional initiative.

**North Star Metric**
The single metric that best captures the core value your product delivers. Everything else is a leading or lagging indicator. Use for: prioritisation, avoiding metric gaming, aligning teams.
