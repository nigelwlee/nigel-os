## Core Thesis

The best strategy is the one you can actually execute with the resources you have. Ambitious positioning only wins if you have the operational discipline to hold it. For a solo operator: ruthless prioritisation over clever strategy — do fewer things, faster, with more conviction.

---

## Key Frameworks

**Diagnosis → Guiding Policy → Coherent Actions** (Rumelt's Good Strategy)
The core of any real strategy. Diagnosis: what is the actual problem? Guiding Policy: the approach that addresses it. Coherent Actions: things that reinforce each other. Most "strategy" is just a list of goals without this logic.

**Jobs to Be Done**
Customers don't buy products, they hire them to do a job. Focus on the job, not the customer segment. The job is stable even as products change — this is your durable product insight.

**Crossing the Chasm (Moore)**
Tech products move: Innovators → Early Adopters → Early Majority → Late Majority → Laggards. The gap between Early Adopters and Early Majority is the chasm. To cross it: target a specific beachhead niche, dominate it completely, use it as a reference market.

**The Pyramid Principle (Minto)**
Structure all communication: conclusion first, then supporting arguments, then evidence. Group supporting points MECE. Every presentation, memo, and recommendation should follow this. The corollary: if you can't state the conclusion first, you don't understand it yet. This principle is embedded in the [[Synthesis]] skill.

**Unit Economics First**
Before any growth move, know: CAC, LTV, payback period, contribution margin. If unit economics are broken, growth makes it worse. Fix the unit, then scale.

**Power (Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers)**
Durable competitive advantage requires one of: Scale Economies, Network Effects, Counter-Positioning, Switching Costs, Branding, Cornered Resource, Process Power. Everything else is temporary. Ask: which of these can this business actually build?

**Two-Pizza Team / Single-Threaded Ownership**
Every initiative needs one person who owns it completely. Committees diffuse accountability. Constrain scope until one person can own it.

## Active Insights

**Speed is a strategy, not just a tactic.** When you're small, speed is your only structural advantage over incumbents. Preserve it aggressively — say no to anything that slows the feedback loop.

**Validation before building.** The cheapest way to test a hypothesis is to sell it before you build it. A landing page, a manual process, a conversation — all valid. The goal is signal, not product.

**Pricing reveals positioning.** How you price tells the market who you're for and what you believe your value is. Underpricing is a signal of insecurity, not accessibility. Charge what you're worth and let it filter.

**The competitor most startups ignore is "do nothing."** Most customers won't adopt your product not because a competitor beat you, but because inertia won. Your job is to make doing nothing feel more painful than switching.

**Strategy is a series of no's.** Every yes to one thing is an implicit no to ten others. The quality of a strategy is judged more by what it excludes than what it includes.

**Context engineering is the new competitive moat.** When everyone accesses the same AI models, organisational context becomes the differentiator (HBR, Feb 2026). For solo founders, accumulated domain expertise and structured knowledge IS the context layer that enterprises spend millions trying to document. The Personal OS is a direct implementation of this — see [[Knowledge/AI/_Briefing-ai|AI Briefing]].

**The operationalise-vs-experiment divide is widening.** The gap between founders who "experiment" with AI and those who have operationalised it into workflows is growing substantially. Winning strategy: master one tool deeply rather than shallow adoption of many. Validates the core thesis — do fewer things, faster, with more conviction.

**Solo founder scale has shifted structurally — 36.3% of all new ventures are now solo-founded, but vertical AI tools are the separating factor.** Up from 23.7% (2019). AI-augmented solo founders reach $100K ARR at 2.5x the rate of non-AI founders. However, vertical micro-tools for legal, finance, healthcare consistently outperform generic AI wrappers. Most solopreneurs who launched AI features report revenue uplifts below 10% — the "billion-dollar one-person company" has not materialised yet (two-person AI-native companies have, but true solo has not). The ceiling is still rising, but the path runs through domain specificity, not generic AI assistants.

**"Prompt vs. subscribe" is replacing "build vs. buy."** AI agents can now replicate core SaaS functionality in hours, and mid-market SaaS companies are seeing churn as customers replace $50-500/month tools with AI-built internal alternatives (Gartner, April 2026). For a solo builder, this cuts both ways: you can replace expensive tool subscriptions with prompted workflows (reducing burn), but your own products face the same substitution risk. Products must deliver value that's hard to replicate with a prompt — proprietary data, network effects, trust/compliance, or real-time integrations.

**Vertical AI agents are winning over horizontal ones.** The AI agent startups gaining real revenue are narrowly vertical — legal document review, medical coding, real estate transaction management — not horizontal "AI assistants" (Sequoia, May 2026). The moat is domain knowledge baked into the system, not AI capability itself. This validates domain-specific tools (iGaming VIP ops, toddler education) over general-purpose AI products.

**Community-Led Growth is replacing PLG as the dominant GTM motion for AI-native products.** When AI makes product features trivially replicable, community (shared identity, peer learning, exclusive access) becomes the defensible moat. Companies with strong communities grow revenue 2.1x faster, with 46% higher LTV and 32% lower CAC. Discord servers, Telegram groups, and paid communities are functioning as both distribution and retention. [[Projects/MY-MYST/_Briefing-my-myst|MY-MYST]] could build a "skeptic mystics" community as a flywheel for content and eventually [[Projects/MY-ASTR/_Briefing-my-astr|Celesto]]. Community isn't a marketing channel — it's the product's immune system against commoditisation.

**Per-seat SaaS pricing is collapsing — but hybrid pricing (base + usage) is winning, not pure outcome-based.** 73% of SaaS companies are actively rebuilding pricing away from per-seat, but 37% are landing on a predictable base subscription plus variable consumption on top (Kyle Poyar's 2026 State of B2B Monetization). Pure outcome-based pricing (as Salesforce and Intercom attempted) has not won. Per-seat revenue share fell from 21% to 15% in 12 months. The implication: price your own products with a base + usage layer, and expect your own SaaS costs to become less predictable. Build for outcomes, not access.

**Services are the new software — the next $1T company sells outcomes, not seats.** Sequoia's thesis (March 2026): six dollars are spent on services for every dollar on software. AI-native services companies combine human judgment with AI execution to deliver outcomes, not tools. Key distinction: "intelligence" tasks (clear right/wrong — coding, accounting) can be fully automated; "judgment" tasks (taste, professional intuition) require human talent plus AI leverage. For a solo builder: the highest-value products sit at the judgment layer, using AI to amplify domain expertise rather than replace it.

**AI API costs dropped 60-80% YoY but total spend grew 320% — architecture choices now matter more than model selection for cost control.** Price floor is $0.25/M input tokens (Gemini Flash-Lite). Prompt caching is the single highest-leverage cost lever: Anthropic offers up to 90% reduction on cached inputs, batch APIs give a further 50% for non-realtime workloads. The implication: for an AI-native operator, caching and batching architecture decisions now dominate model selection in determining actual costs. Volume will eat any per-unit savings.
