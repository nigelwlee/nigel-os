## Core Thesis

Taste is a point of view held with conviction. Great creative work isn't the result of following best practices — it's the result of having a clear perspective on what matters, what's worth making, and what is boring. The rarest skill in creative work is the courage to be specific.

---

## Key Frameworks

**The Single Overriding Communication Objective (SOCO)**
Every piece of creative work has one job. One. Not three, not five. If you can't state what it is in one sentence, it will fail. Everything else — visual, copy, format — serves that one objective. Use for: briefs, campaigns, product copy, any output that needs to communicate.

**The Remarkable / Useful / Beautiful Triangle**
Good creative is at least one of these; great creative is two or more. Remarkable: worth talking about. Useful: solves a real problem. Beautiful: aesthetically compelling. Most corporate creative hits none of these.

**Reference Before Execution**
Never brief creative work without a reference library. References communicate taste faster and more accurately than words. "Bold but warm" means nothing. A mood board of six images means everything. Use for: all creative briefs, design direction, brand work.

**Taste as a Filter, Not a Generator**
Taste is not about generating original ideas — it is about knowing which ideas to keep. Quantity first ([[Jam]]), then brutal filtering ([[Synthesis]]). The filter is the skill. Use for: content strategy, editing your own work, curating.

**Brand as a Person**
Define a brand by asking: if this brand were a person, who would they be? What would they wear? What would they refuse to say? What music would they listen to? The person answer is more useful than a brand pyramid. Use for: brand strategy, voice and tone, visual direction.

## Active Insights

**Specificity is the engine of good writing.** "A fast car" is forgettable. "A car that corners at 0.9g" is not. The more specific the detail, the more real the work feels. Generalities are what people say when they haven't decided yet.

**The headline is 80% of the work.** Most people read the headline and nothing else. Invest there first. A mediocre idea with a great headline outperforms a great idea with a mediocre one.

**Constraints unlock creativity.** The blank page is the enemy. Give yourself a constraint (one colour, one typeface, 140 characters) and the creative problem becomes solvable. Arbitrary constraints are still useful.

**The first version is always worse than the tenth.** Creative quality is a function of iteration count, not innate inspiration. The edit is where quality happens. Ship version one fast; make version ten good.

**Boredom is a signal.** If you're bored making it, the audience will be bored consuming it. When something feels boring in production, it's not "safe" — it's already failing.

**Platform context shapes everything.** The same content performs differently depending on where and when it's seen. Design for the feed, the scroll, the notification. Context is not a constraint to work around; it is part of the medium.

**Affinity Designer is the production tool for SVG/digital asset creation.** From [[Projects/MY-SSTU/_Briefing-my-sstu|Sleepy Studios]]: layer exclusion from noise effects, transparent PNG export, and Cricut-compatible SVG output are recurring workflows. Affinity's non-destructive workflow supports rapid iteration on card templates.

**AI character consistency is largely solved — 85-92% is the practical ceiling.** Reference-based generation (FLUX 2 Multi-Reference, GPT Image 2, Leonardo Phoenix 2.0) now maintains character identity across poses and scenes without manual conversion pipelines. The "triple stack" workflow (IP-Adapter FaceID + character LoRA + ControlNet) in ComfyUI handles professional needs. Budget 10-15% cleanup time for the remaining drift — full automation is still not there, but the workflow has shifted from "unsolved" to "production-ready with polish."

**Brand anti-patterns are as important as brand guidelines.** From [[Projects/RB-NETW/_Briefing-rb-netw|Tokeniser]]: explicitly listing what a brand will *never* do (no chain links, no hexagons, no neon) is more useful than aspirational positives. Anti-patterns are concrete and testable; "bold but warm" is neither.

**Celebration-first design drives retention in kids' apps.** From [[Projects/MY-DIDIT/_Briefing-my-didit|Did·It]]: confetti, success screens, and milestone triggers (not scores or rankings) create the emotional reward loop. No fail states means every interaction ends positively. The celebration *is* the retention mechanic.

**The unlikely-profile persona is a content differentiator.** From [[Projects/MY-MYST/_Briefing-my-myst|Mystic Scientist]]: positioning as "the last person you'd expect to get into this" creates immediate tension and curiosity. An ex-consultant exploring astrology is inherently more watchable than another astrology creator because the skeptic journey *is* the content. The persona contradiction (analytical + mystical) does the marketing work — no need to explain why your take is different.

**Human-authored content receives 5.44x more traffic than AI-generated content — and the gap is widening.** Consumer preference for AI-generated content dropped from 60% (2023) to 26% (2026). AI content shows volatile traffic patterns over 5 months while human content grows steadily. 83% of consumers can spot AI videos; 36% say it lowers brand trust. The practical takeaway: use AI to accelerate production but maintain a recognisably human voice and editorial layer. For [[Projects/MY-MYST/_Briefing-my-myst|MY-MYST]] and [[Projects/MY-YAP/_Briefing-my-yap|MY-YAP]]: the talking-head, personal-journey format is a strategic advantage, not a production compromise.

**Platform AI disclosure rules now have real enforcement teeth.** TikTok: 3 unlabeled AI videos → 60% reach reduction for 30 days + Creator Fund suspension. EU AI Act Article 50 (effective August 2, 2026) requires machine-readable marking of all synthetic content — fines up to EUR 15M. YouTube and Meta require manual flagging. This isn't ethics — it's risk management. Build disclosure into the production workflow from day one.

**Contrarian hooks with personal stakes outperform generic takes.** From [[Projects/MY-YAP/_Briefing-my-yap|MY-YAP]]: "I don't think kids should go to school" works because it's specific, polarising, and backed by a personal stake (3.5-year-old child). Generic takes ("education needs reform") don't create scroll-stopping tension. The hook formula: contrarian claim + personal reason to care + specific examples that make the audience reconsider.

**Figma shipped Motion and Code Layers (Config 2026, June 24) — collapsing design-to-production into a single agent-compatible tool chain.** Figma Motion adds timeline keyframes and exports to Animated SVG, CSS, JSON, React, MP4, WebM, and GIF. Both Motion and Code Layers are MCP-compatible — animated designs pipe directly to a coding agent for implementation. Code Layers (beta July 2026) let you clone a GitHub repo onto the canvas and convert layers to interactive React-backed layers with one click or a prompt. For a solo builder, this collapses design-to-code into a single tool with agent handoff built in. Free in open beta.

**The dominant 2026 design trend is "anti-AI perfectionism" — deliberate imperfection, tactile textures, and motion-first identity systems.** Brands like Duolingo, Mailchimp, and Oatly normalised it. 68% of Gen Z consumers prefer "playful, imperfect" visuals. Audiences are fatigued by the polished sameness of AI-generated visuals — brands differentiating with personality, organic textures, and kinetic/adaptive logos outperform rigid, hyper-clean identities. For SVG product creation at [[Projects/MY-SSTU/_Briefing-my-sstu|Sleepy Studios]]: wobbly lines, visible sketch marks, and textured fills will outperform clean vector perfection. For brand building: this is permission to lean into distinctiveness over polish.

## Aesthetic Positions (Nigel's)

These are held positions, not universal rules:

- **Dense > sparse** in writing. Don't pad. Every word earns its place.
- **Specific > abstract** in both words and visuals.
- **Understated > loud** in brand identity. Quiet confidence over shouting.
- **Functional beauty** — things that look good because they work well, not despite it.
- **References matter** — work that knows its influences is more interesting than work that doesn't.

## Open Questions / Active Debates

- How do you maintain creative quality when velocity is high? What gets sacrificed?
- When does consistency become conservatism? How do you evolve a brand without losing it?
- What is the right balance between AI-assisted and human-originated creative work? Emerging data: AI works best as production accelerator (drafting, editing, scheduling), but audience-facing "voice" and persona must stay human. Simply adding AI tools without restructuring workflows can increase burnout rather than relieve it (HBR, Feb 2026).
- How should new YouTube channels sequence Shorts vs long-form? Emerging consensus: Shorts for discovery (10x reach, 5x lower conversion), long-form for monetisation (50-100x higher RPM). 30-40% Shorts / 60-70% long-form is the recommended split.
